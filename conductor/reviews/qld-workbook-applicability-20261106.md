# Queensland historical workbook applicability — 6 November 2026

The workbook's Queensland salary matches do not by themselves identify an
applicable agreement. The retained [General Employees award reprint operative
1 March 2023](https://www.qirc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/hospital_health_010323.pdf),
clause 4 and Schedule 2 (PDF pages 5–6 and 48–49), describes covered employers
and employees and lists indicative occupational groupings. Scientist appears
under the professional stream; laboratory assistant appears under the
operational stream. These are indicative groupings, not a classification decision
for a named person or proof that a workbook's laboratory technician is a
laboratory assistant.

The [Queensland Public Health Sector Certified Agreement No. 11](https://www.qirc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-06/2023_cb41.pdf),
clauses 1.4–1.7 (PDF page 8), ties its application to specified employer parties
and employees with prescribed classifications and rates. It also describes its
relationship to the named awards and a specified inconsistency rule. Certification,
commencement, wage-effective and pay-period dates must remain distinct.

The separately retained [Health Practitioners and Dental Officers Agreement No. 4](https://www.qirc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-06/2023_cb43.pdf),
clauses 1.4–1.7 (PDF page 7), also applies to specified employer parties and
employees with prescribed classifications and rates. It identifies the HPDO award
for health practitioners and dental officers, and the General Employees award
for clinical assistants, with transitional arrangements. Therefore the presence
of the General Employees award alone does not settle which agreement covers a
clinical assistant; nor does the generic word scientist prove an HP2–3 appointment.

For the workbook medical-scientist and bioinformatics rows, the earlier salary
comparison supports a General Employees professional schedule candidate but
conflicts with the HP2–3 label on one row. Resolving the selection requires the
actual employer, appointment/classification and pay point, duties/stream, worker
category and relevant period, followed by a check of the applicable instrument
and any variation. The workbook does not provide those facts. No candidate has
been promoted to an accepted rate or entitlement. These historical instruments
and nominal expiry dates are not evidence of the currently applicable agreement.

## Laboratory discipline and hours — 6 November 2026

Schedule 3 of the Health Practitioners and Dental Officers agreement (PDF page
61) expressly lists medical laboratory scientists and technicians among its
eligible disciplines. This makes that agreement a concrete candidate for the
workbook's laboratory and scientist descriptions, subject to its application,
appointment and date requirements. It does not establish that either workbook
row actually represents an employee covered by it, nor that the separate
General Employees salary endpoints belong to the HP salary scale.

In the General Employees award reprint, clause 15.1 (PDF page 29) specifies
average ordinary hours for full-time employees and refers to exceptions in
Schedule 8. Schedule 8 (PDF page 75) preserves a 36.25-hour week for specified
scientists, technicians, scientific assistants and cadets employed in pathology
on 1 January 2015, subject to the stated continuity conditions. The workbook
uses 36.25 in `Labour - Prices!K22:K25` for laboratory, medical scientist,
administration and bioinformatics rows. Those role labels alone do not establish
Schedule 8 eligibility, pathology employment or continuity, and the
administration and bioinformatics labels do not by themselves identify one of
the specified occupational categories. Equally, this 2023 reprint does not
prove what the correct condition was on every 2022 date.

The twelve formulas in `AB22:AD25` divide annual totals by 52 and the respective
column K value. Holding annual cost fixed, 36.25 rather than 38 hours increases
the calculated hourly output by `38/36.25 - 1`, approximately 4.83%. This is
an arithmetic sensitivity, not a direction to replace either denominator or a
finding about an individual's award. Verify source, worker category, employment
and continuity history, applicable period and the intended denominator before
accepting a condition.

## Health practitioner award in the workbook base year

The [Health Practitioners and Dental Officers award reprint operative
1 September 2022](https://www.qirc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-09/health_practitioners_010922.pdf)
provides a base-year comparator independent of the later agreement. Its clause
4 and Schedule 2 (PDF pages 6 and 42) describe covered health-service employees
and list medical laboratory scientists and technicians among eligible health
practitioner disciplines, subject to the schedule's role definition. Clause
15.1(a)–(b) (PDF page 23) sets an average 38 ordinary hours per week and work
cycles for employees covered by that award. Clause 15.1(d) separately permits
specified agreed daily arrangements; the source must be read as a whole.

The workbook's Queensland laboratory and medical-scientist rows use 36.25 hours
per week and cite the General Employees award, while the scientist row also
carries an HP2–3 label. If either row represents a worker covered by this 2022
HPDO award and its ordinary-hours measure is the intended denominator, 36.25
does not match its stated 38-hour average. That conditional finding is stronger
than assuming the General Employees pathology exception applies. It does not
establish the actual appointment, employer, role duties, worker-specific roster,
applicable agreement or author intent, and it is not an instruction to change
the original workbook. The 2023 certified agreement's separate certification
and pay-effective dates remain as previously documented.
