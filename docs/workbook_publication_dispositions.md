# Archived publication displays and scalar labels

The pinned original workbook's publication sheet contains both numeric hourly
bounds and literal formatted displays. Thirty flattened display strings have an
exact correspondence with the retained native results of thirty neighbouring
format formulas. Thirty wide display cells also match those results when joined
by the original profession, jurisdiction and sensitivity labels.

This supports candidate archival display associations. Equal strings and source
formula correspondence do not prove a historical copy operation, validate the
numeric bounds, or establish statistical confidence intervals or Monte Carlo
results. The numeric source columns retain their existing separate meanings.

The complete candidate profile now gives the flattened literal strings a
`presentation` role and `flattened_hourly_bounds_display` name. Their numeric
unit remains null because they are composite text, not accepted numeric values.
The adjacent five profession and five variable labels have explicit identity
roles. Original strings, including their formatting and any source discrepancies,
remain unchanged; they are not parsed into pay rates.

The declared column that has no populated cells in the pinned lower layout has
an `unpopulated_layout` disposition. This records physical emptiness in that
range, not the author's intended future use. It does not create a missing
observation, a zero or a numerical unit.

On the PSA validation sheet, the scalar's title is a label/header. The following
scalar retains `unresolved_historical_value` status with an unknown unit and
estimand. A title does not make it an accepted PSA oracle. The isolated zero on
the DSA sheet also remains unresolved. Assumed units and all scientific/source
qualification gaps remain explicit, including the retained failed archive
interpretation diagnostics. No numeric costing or stochastic method changes.
